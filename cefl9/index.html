Getting Hired

The job market is changing fast, and the people who get hired know how to show what they're worth. In this unit you'll decode job postings, build a personal brand, tailor your résumé, find where the jobs are, manage your digital identity and speak up for yourself in an interview.

2Lessons

1Decoding a job postingWhat are they really asking for?›
I can read a job posting and pick out the skills and qualifications it asks for. 9.1
Job postings list qualifications (training or certificates you need) and skills (what you can do). Highlight both, then match them to your own.

Example A real-style posting

Front Counter Staff · Part-time
• Friendly and comfortable talking with customers
• Able to handle cash and make change
• Reliable: available weekends
• Food handler certificate an asset
• Must be 15 or older
  • Transferable skills: customer service, handling cash, reliability
  • Qualifications: age 15+, food handler certificate ("an asset" means nice to have, not required)

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Posting Match

Find 2 real job postings on Job Bank for jobs a 15–16-year-old could do. Highlight the skills and qualifications, then list which ones you already have.

2Your personal brandWho you are in one or two sentences›
I can write a personal brand statement. 9.1
Your personal brand is how you want others to see your skills, values and goals. A brand statement sums it up in 1–2 sentences.

Formula

I am a [quality] person who [skill or strength]. I want to [goal].

Examples

  • "I'm a hard-working, hands-on learner who loves fixing things. I want to become a heavy-duty mechanic."
  • "I'm a patient, caring person who's great with kids. I'm working toward a career in early childhood education."
Use your brand statement at the top of your résumé, in your cover letter and to answer "Tell me about yourself."

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Brand Check

Write your brand statement, then ask 3 people who know you if it sounds like you. Revise it using their feedback.

3Tailoring your résuméOne résumé doesn't fit every job›
I can tailor my résumé to a specific opportunity. 9.1
Your résumé shows your human capital: the education, skills and experience that make you valuable. Tailoring means moving the most relevant parts to the top and using the posting's own words.

Example Same person, two jobs

For a daycare helper jobFor a hardware store job
Lead with: babysitting, coaching kids' soccer, first aidLead with: building a shed with family, handling cash at the class store
Same person, but each résumé puts the best match first.

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Two Versions

Make 2 versions of your résumé for 2 very different jobs. Highlight what you changed and why.

4Where are the jobs?Search tools, networks and services›
I can use job search tools, networks and employment services. 9.3
Many jobs are never posted online. Use every route: job websites, employer websites, job fairs, your network, and free employment services.

Routes to a job

  • Online: Job Bank, ALIS, company websites
  • In person: "Now hiring" signs, dropping off a résumé, job fairs
  • Your network: family, friends, coaches, teachers, neighbours
  • Employment services: free help with résumés and job searches, including services for youth, newcomers to Canada and Francophone job seekers

Example

Priya told her soccer coach she was looking for summer work. The coach's sister ran a garden centre and needed help. Priya got hired before the job was ever posted.

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Job Map

Make a map or list of 10 places within walking or bus distance of home that hire teens. Note how each one hires: online, in person, or through people you know.

5Your digital identityEmployers look you up›
I can explain the risks and benefits of my digital identity. 9.3
Your digital identity is everything about you online: posts, photos, comments, usernames and what others post about you. Many employers search applicants' names before hiring.
RisksBenefits
Rude comments or bullying postsShowing off your art, sports or projects
Photos of unsafe or illegal behaviourVolunteering and community involvement
Sharing too much personal informationA professional profile (when you're older)

Quick check-up

  • Search your own name. What comes up?
  • Set personal accounts to private.
  • Ask: Would I be OK with my future boss seeing this?

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Digital Clean-Up

Search your name online with an adult, set your accounts to private, and remove anything you wouldn't want an employer to see. Write a short reflection on what you found.

6Interviews and self-advocacySpeak up for your value›
I can practise interviews and advocate for myself. 9.1
An interview is your chance to show your human capital. Self-advocacy means clearly explaining your skills, your value and what you need to do your best work.

Self-advocacy examples

  • Your value: "I'm the person my team counts on to show up on time, every time."
  • Your needs: "I learn best when I can see a task done once first. Could someone show me on my first shift?"
  • Your availability: "I can work weekends, but I have hockey Tuesday nights."
Being clear and honest about what you need is professional, not weak.

Before the interview

  • Practise "Tell me about yourself" using your brand statement.
  • Have one story ready for teamwork, solving a problem and a mistake you learned from.
  • Plan your route and arrive 10 minutes early.

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Panel Practice

Do a mock interview with the Grade 8 panel, then interview a Grade 8 student about their goals so they can practise answering too.
