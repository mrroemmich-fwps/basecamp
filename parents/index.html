2How the class works

🧭 Real-world learning

Every subject connects to life at home, at work and in the community: budgets and paycheques, job applications, safety at work, reading the news and more.

⛰️ Units on a trail

Each course is a trail of units. Each unit has learning goals, short lessons with worked examples, practice, resources and tips for families.

💼 Careers and money

Our school is piloting Alberta's new Career Education and Financial Literacy (CEFL) course, about jobs, pay, taxes, saving, credit and planning after high school.

3Learning tools your child can use at home

🎯
Practice sets

Ten questions from a unit, one at a time, with hints and the worked answer after two tries. Find them in the Practice section of every unit.

✅
Am I ready?

A short check before a test. It scores the answers and lists the lessons to review.

🃏
Flashcards

Key words for each unit. Great for quick quizzing at the kitchen table.

🧭
Real-world tasks

A practical problem for each unit, like planning a budget or writing an email to a manager. Your child writes an answer, then compares it with a sample.

🖨️
Worksheets

Print a practice sheet for any unit if your child likes working on paper.

🔊
Read aloud and larger text

The round buttons in the bottom-right corner of every course page read lessons and questions aloud and make the text bigger.

Three levels, one skill

Practice comes in three levels. Students can pick the one that fits today and move up when they're ready.

  • 🥾 Base Camp: a hint on every question, smaller numbers in math, and a calculator is always OK.
  • 🌲 Climb: everyday questions, with a hint after one wrong try.
  • 🏔️ Summit: harder questions and no hints.

If your child has an Individualized Program Plan (IPP), these tools can match many of their supports. Ask me which level and tools to use at home.

4Helping at home

Every day

  • Ask "What did you learn today?" and then "Can you show me?" Teaching you is great practice.
  • Keep a regular routine for sleep, homework and screen time.
  • Praise effort and progress, not just marks. Mistakes are part of learning.

Math and money

  • Let your child estimate the grocery total, count change or compare unit prices.
  • Cook together and let them do the measuring and the fractions.
  • Share a pay stub or bill (cover private info) and find the numbers together.

Reading, writing and media

  • Read anything together: recipes, sports stories, manuals, the news.
  • Ask "Is that fact or opinion?" about ads and posts.
  • Have your child write real messages: a thank-you, an email to a coach.

Careers and the future

  • Talk about the jobs you and relatives have had: how you got them and what skills they needed.
  • Explore careers together on ALIS, Alberta's career site.
  • Encourage volunteering, chores and part-time work. They build real job skills.

Every unit page also ends with a short "For parents" box with ideas that match that unit.

5Avalanche HQ and Expeditions

🏔️ Avalanche HQ

Our class reward system, designed with the students. They earn a pretend currency called AvCash for effort, teamwork and kindness. They can spend it in the class store (snacks, tea, game time and more) or put it into pretend "local" investments to watch it grow. No real money is involved.

🧭 Expeditions

Optional side quests that connect learning to home and community, like measuring a room, interviewing a family member about their first job, or helping a neighbour. Students earn extra AvCash for themselves or others. Many Expeditions are perfect to do together as a family.

Students sign in to Avalanche HQ with a nickname and PIN they choose. No real names, emails or school accounts are used.

6Questions families ask

Does my child need an account to use the site?

No. All the lessons and practice are open to everyone. Only Avalanche HQ uses a sign-in, with a nickname and PIN.

Does it work on a phone or tablet?

Yes. The site works on phones, tablets, Chromebooks and computers.

Is any of my child's information stored on this site?

No. Practice results aren't saved or sent anywhere. A device may remember small settings like the chosen level or larger text.

Why are some units marked "Coming soon"?

Units open as the class reaches them during the year. Open units stay open for review.

What is CEFL?

Career Education and Financial Literacy is a new Alberta course for Grades 7 to 9. Our school is piloting it this year. It covers careers, pay and taxes, budgeting, saving and investing, credit and debt, and planning after high school.

My child is stuck on homework. Where should they start?

Open the unit, reread the lesson's big idea and examples, then try the Practice set at the Base Camp level. If they're still stuck, have them bring the question to class or contact me.
