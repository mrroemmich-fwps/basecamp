Unit 2

Integers & Temperature

In an Alberta winter, numbers go below zero all the time. In this unit you'll use positive and negative numbers, read thermometers, figure out how much the temperature changed, and switch between Celsius and Fahrenheit.

1Positive and negative numbersAbove and below zero›
I can describe where positive and negative numbers are used. Number 14
Positive numbers are above zero. Negative numbers are below zero and have a minus sign: −5. Positive and negative whole numbers are called integers.

2The number lineOrdering integers›
I can order positive and negative numbers on a number line. Number 14
On a number line, numbers get bigger to the right and smaller to the left. A thermometer is just a number line standing up.

Example Which is colder?

−7°C or −3°C? −7 is farther left on the number line, so −7°C is colder. With negatives, the bigger the number after the minus sign, the colder it is.
3Temperature changesRising and falling›
I can find a new temperature after it rises or falls. Number 13, 14
Rising means move up (right) on the number line. Falling means move down (left). Crossing zero is fine; just keep counting.

Example 1 Rising past zero

It's −6°C and rises 10°. Count up 6 to reach 0, then 4 more: 4°C.

Example 2 Falling below zero

It's 3°C and falls 8°. Count down 3 to reach 0, then 5 more: −5°C.
4Temperature differencesHow far apart?›
I can find the difference between two temperatures. Number 13, 14
To find the difference between a negative and a positive temperature, count to zero, then count past it, and add the two parts.

Example A chinook

A chinook wind blows in and the temperature goes from −18°C to 7°C.
  1. −18 up to 0 is 18 degrees.
  2. 0 up to 7 is 7 degrees.
  3. 18 + 7 = 25 degrees warmer

Integers & Temperature Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Math

Integers are positive and negative whole numbers. Negative = below zero (−5). Zero is neither.

Number line: bigger to the right, smaller to the left. −7 is colder than −3.

Rising: count up (right). Falling: count down (left). Crossing zero: count to 0, then keep going.

Difference between − and +: count to zero, count past zero, add the two parts. −18 to 7 = 18 + 7 = 25°.
