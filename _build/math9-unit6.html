Unit 6

Paycheques & Time

Your first job means time cards, hourly wages, maybe overtime, and a pay stub full of deductions. In this unit you'll add up hours and minutes, calculate gross pay, compare ways of getting paid, and figure out what you actually take home.

1Adding hours and minutes60 minutes make an hour›
I can add and subtract hours and minutes. Shape & Space 11
Add the hours, then add the minutes. Every 60 minutes trades for 1 hour. To use time in a pay calculation, change minutes to a decimal: 15 min = 0.25 h, 30 min = 0.5 h, 45 min = 0.75 h.

Example 1 A time card

Mon 4 h 45 min + Tue 5 h 30 min + Wed 6 h 15 min.
Hours: 4 + 5 + 6 = 15. Minutes: 45 + 30 + 15 = 90 = 1 h 30 min.
Total: 16 h 30 min = 16.5 hours.

Example 2 Subtracting

A shift is 8 h. You've worked 5 h 40 min. Trade 1 hour: 8 h = 7 h 60 min. 7 h 60 min − 5 h 40 min = 2 h 20 min left.
2Hourly payHours × wage›
I can calculate pay from an hourly wage and a time card. Number 5, 10
A wage is a rate: dollars per hour. gross pay = hours worked × hourly wage. Gross pay is what you earn before anything is taken off.

Example Grocery clerk

16.5 hours at $16.25 an hour: 16.5 × 16.25 = $268.13 (rounded to the cent).
3OvertimeTime and a half›
I can calculate overtime pay. Number 5, 10
In Alberta, most workers earn overtime for hours over 8 in a day or 44 in a week, whichever is more. Overtime is usually paid at 1.5 times the regular wage ("time and a half").

Example A busy week

You earn $18 an hour and work 48 hours this week.
Regular: 44 × $18 = $792
Overtime rate: 1.5 × $18 = $27. Overtime: 4 × $27 = $108
Gross pay: $792 + $108 = $900

Example Which pays more?

Job A: $16 an hour for 40 hours. Job B: $300 a week plus 4% commission on $9,000 in sales.
A: 16 × 40 = $640. B: 300 + 0.04 × 9,000 = 300 + 360 = $660. Job B pays $20 more this week, but if sales drop, it could pay less.
5The pay stubGross pay vs. net pay›
I can read a pay stub and find net pay. Number 5, 9
Gross pay is what you earn. Deductions are taken off. Net pay is what lands in your bank account. net pay = gross pay − deductions.

Example A sample pay stub

Hours: 32 at $16.00Gross pay: $512.00
CPP (Canada Pension Plan)−$26.00
EI (Employment Insurance)−$8.40
Income tax−$41.20
Net pay$436.40
These deduction amounts are examples. Real amounts depend on how much you earn.

Gross pay = hours × hourly wage.
