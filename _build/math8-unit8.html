Unit 8

Perimeter & Area

Fencing a yard, laying flooring, painting a wall, planning a room: tradespeople measure all day. In this unit you'll measure in metric and imperial, find perimeter and area, work with circles, and draw a floor plan on a grid.

1Measuring lengthMetric and imperial›
I can measure length in metric and imperial units and convert with a chart. Shape & Space 1–3, 9
Canada mostly uses metric (mm, cm, m, km), but lumber, tools and many trades still use imperial (inches, feet, yards, miles).

Example A 2 × 4

A board labelled 8 ft long: 8 × 30.48 = about 244 cm, or 2.44 m.
2PerimeterThe distance around›
I can find the perimeter of rectangles and triangles. Shape & Space 4
Perimeter is the distance around a shape. Add up all the sides. For a rectangle: P = 2 × (length + width).

Example Fencing a dog run

A dog run is 12 m by 5 m. Perimeter = 12 + 5 + 12 + 5 = 34 m of fencing. If fencing costs $9 a metre, that's 34 × 9 = $306.
3AreaThe space inside›
I can find the area of rectangles and irregular shapes. Shape & Space 5
Area is the space inside a shape, in square units (m², cm²). Rectangle: A = length × width. Irregular shape: split it into rectangles and add.

Example Flooring an L-shaped room

A = 8 × 3 = 24 m². B = 4 × 2.5 = 10 m². Total = 34 m² of flooring. Installers buy about 10% extra for cuts.
4CirclesRadius, diameter, circumference›
I can explain how radius, diameter and circumference relate. Shape & Space 6
The radius goes from the centre to the edge. The diameter goes all the way across: d = 2 × r. The circumference is the distance around: C ≈ 3.14 × d.

Example A round table

A table has a diameter of 120 cm. Edging around it: 3.14 × 120 = about 377 cm.
5Grids, symmetry and floor plansPlan a space on paper›
I can use grids, symmetry and transformations to plan a space. Shape & Space 15–22
On a grid, points are named with ordered pairs (x, y): across first, then up. A shape has symmetry if it folds into matching halves. Shapes can slide (translate), turn (rotate) or flip (reflect). Shapes that cover a floor with no gaps make a tessellation.

Example A bedroom floor plan

On a grid where each square is 1 m, a bed is placed with corners at (1, 1), (3, 1), (3, 3) and (1, 3). It's 2 m by 2 m. Sliding it 2 squares right moves it to (3, 1), (5, 1), (5, 3), (3, 3).

Perimeter & Area Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Math

Metric: 10 mm = 1 cm · 100 cm = 1 m · 1,000 m = 1 km. Imperial: 12 in = 1 ft · 3 ft = 1 yd. Chart: 1 in ≈ 2.54 cm · 1 ft ≈ 30.48 cm · 1 mi ≈ 1.6 km

Perimeter (distance around): add all sides. Rectangle: P = 2 × (l + w).

Area (space inside): rectangle A = l × w, in square units (m²). Irregular: split into rectangles and add.

Circles: d = 2 × r · C ≈ 3.14 × d

Grids: (x, y) = across, then up. Translate = slide · rotate = turn · reflect = flip. Tessellation = shapes covering a surface with no gaps.
