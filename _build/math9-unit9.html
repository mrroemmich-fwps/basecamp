Unit 9

Circles & 3-D Objects

Tires, pipes, cans, boxes and buildings: the world is full of circles and 3-D shapes. In this unit you'll work with circumference, measure and convert in metric and imperial, find volume and surface area, sketch 3-D objects, and look for symmetry.

1CirclesRadius, diameter and circumference›
I can find the circumference, radius and diameter of a circle. Shape & Space 8
diameter = 2 × radius. circumference ≈ 3.14 × diameter. The number 3.14… is called pi (π). Working backwards: diameter ≈ circumference ÷ 3.14.

Example 1 A tire

A tire has a diameter of 65 cm. In one turn it rolls 3.14 × 65 ≈ 204 cm, about 2 m.

Example 2 Working backwards

A tree trunk measures 157 cm around. Its diameter is 157 ÷ 3.14 = 50 cm.

3Volume and surface areaInside and outside of a box›
I can find the volume and surface area of a box. Shape & Space 1
Volume is how much fits inside: V = length × width × height (cm³ or m³). Surface area is the area of all the outside faces added up. A box has 6 faces in 3 matching pairs.

Example A shipping box: 30 cm × 20 cm × 10 cm

Volume: 30 × 20 × 10 = 6,000 cm³ (6 L, since 1,000 cm³ = 1 L).
Surface area: top/bottom 2 × (30 × 20) = 1,200, front/back 2 × (30 × 10) = 600, sides 2 × (20 × 10) = 400. Total = 2,200 cm² of cardboard.
43-D objects and sketchesFaces, edges and skeletons›
I can classify, build and sketch 3-D objects. Shape & Space 12, 13
Prisms have two matching ends joined by rectangles. Pyramids have one base and triangles that meet at a point. A skeleton is just the edges, like a frame of toothpicks. To sketch a box, draw two matching rectangles offset a bit and join the corners.
5Symmetry and designsFlips, slides and turns›
I can find lines of symmetry and use transformations in designs. Shape & Space 14, 15, 18
A line of symmetry splits a shape into mirror halves. A regular polygon has as many lines of symmetry as it has sides. Designers use reflections (flips), translations (slides) and rotations (turns) to make logos, quilts and tile patterns.
