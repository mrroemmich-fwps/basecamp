Unit 4

The Aztecs & Spain

In 1519, two very different worldviews met in Mexico: the powerful Aztec Empire and Spanish explorers. In just two years, Tenochtitlan, one of the largest cities in the world, fell. In this unit you'll explore Aztec life, why Spain expanded, what happened at contact, and how it changed the Aztec people forever.

1The Aztec worldTenochtitlan and daily life›
I can describe the Aztec worldview and how it showed in daily life. 8.3 · CC, I
The Aztecs (Mexica) built Tenochtitlan on an island in Lake Texcoco around 1325. It had causeways, canals, markets and floating gardens called chinampas. Their worldview centred on the gods, who they believed kept the sun moving and crops growing, and on duty to the community. Conquered peoples paid tribute (goods) to the empire.
Part of lifeHow the worldview showed
ReligionHuge temples, festivals and offerings to gods like Huitzilopochtli
LivelihoodFarming maize on chinampas, busy markets
GovernmentAn emperor, nobles and priests; tribute from conquered cities
FamilyEvery child went to school; duty to family and community
2Spain expandsGold, God and glory›
I can explain what drove Spain to explore and expand. 8.3 · ER, GC
After 1492, Spain's worldview pushed it outward. Historians often sum up the reasons as "gold, God and glory": wealth from new lands, spreading Christianity, and fame and power for Spain and its explorers. Spain believed it had the right to claim lands for its king. This is called imperialism.
3Contact and conquest1519 to 1521›
I can explain how technology, allies and disease led to Spanish dominance. 8.3 · TCC
Hernán Cortés landed in Mexico in 1519 with a few hundred soldiers. By 1521, Tenochtitlan had fallen. How?
FactorWhat happened
DiseaseSmallpox, new to the Americas, killed huge numbers of people, including leaders
AlliesThousands of warriors from peoples like the Tlaxcalans, who were enemies of the Aztecs, joined the Spanish
TechnologySteel swords, armour, guns, cannons and horses
TranslationMalintzin (La Malinche), an Indigenous woman, translated and advised Cortés
4After contactA changed identity›
I can describe how Aztec beliefs and identity changed after contact. 8.3 · I, CC
Spain built Mexico City on top of Tenochtitlan. Temples were torn down and churches built. People were forced to work for Spanish landowners, and many converted to Christianity. Yet much survived: the Nahuatl language is still spoken by over a million people, and words like chocolate, tomato and avocado come from it. Today's Mexican identity blends Indigenous and Spanish cultures.

Aztecs & Spain Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Social Studies

Tenochtitlan: Aztec capital built on Lake Texcoco around 1325. Chinampas = floating gardens. Tribute = goods paid by conquered peoples.

Spain's motives: gold (wealth) · God (spread Christianity) · glory (fame, power). Imperialism: taking control of other lands and peoples.

Contact: Cortés arrives 1519 · Tenochtitlan falls 1521.

Why Spain won: disease (smallpox) · Indigenous allies (Tlaxcalans) · technology (steel, guns, horses) · translation (Malintzin).

After: Mexico City built on the ruins · forced labour and conversion · Nahuatl survives · blended identity.
