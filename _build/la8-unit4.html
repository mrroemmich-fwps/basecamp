Unit 4

Media Literacy

Ads, news, videos, websites and posts are all built by someone, for a reason. In this unit you'll read everyday media with a purpose, tell fact from opinion, check whether a source can be trusted, and spot the tricks advertisers use.

2Lessons

1Reading everyday textsSchedules, labels and websites›
I can read everyday texts like schedules, labels and websites for a purpose. 2.1.3
Everyday texts are built to be scanned, not read top to bottom. Know your purpose first ("What time is the bus?"), then use headings, bold words, tables, icons and menus to jump to the answer.

Example A bus schedule

StopRun 1Run 2Run 3
Main St7:107:408:10
Library7:187:488:18
School7:308:008:30
You need to be at school by 8:15. Find "School," look for the latest time before 8:15: 8:00. Go up that column: catch the 7:40 bus at Main St.
2Fact or opinion?Can it be proven?›
I can tell fact from opinion. 3.2.2
A fact can be proven true or false. An opinion is what someone believes or feels. Opinion clue words: best, worst, should, amazing, I think, everyone knows.

Examples

Fact: The Oilers play their home games in Edmonton.
Opinion: The Oilers have the best fans in the league.
Tricky: "Most people agree this is the best phone." Still an opinion, just dressed up.
3Can I trust it?Checking sources›
I can check whether a source is credible and current. 3.2.3, 3.3.3
Before you believe or share something, check it. Ask: Who made it? When? Why (to inform, sell, entertain, persuade)? Can I find the same information from another trusted source?

Example

A video says a new pill "melts belly fat overnight." Who made it? The company selling the pill. Why? To sell. Do doctors or health sites agree? No. Not credible.

Media Literacy Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Language Arts

Everyday texts: know your purpose, then scan headings, bold words, tables and icons.

Fact = can be proven. Opinion = a belief or feeling. Clue words: best, worst, should, amazing, I think.

Check a source: Who made it? When? Why? Can I confirm it somewhere trusted?

Media: TV (video, emotion) · radio (listen anywhere) · print (detail) · online (fast, but false info spreads).

Ad techniques: bandwagon · celebrity · expert · urgency · emotion · glittering words.
