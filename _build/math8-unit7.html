Unit 7

Patterns & Equations

Patterns help you predict: how much you'll save, how many tiles you'll need, what you'll earn by Friday. In this unit you'll find pattern rules, use variables to describe real situations, and solve simple equations by keeping them balanced.

1Patterns all aroundNature, buildings, money›
I can describe patterns in nature and everyday life. Patterns 1
A pattern repeats or grows in a predictable way. Look for what changes and what stays the same.

Examples

  • Nature: a snowflake has 6 matching arms. Many flowers have 3, 5 or 8 petals.
  • Building: bricks in a wall alternate in rows.
  • Money: saving $5 a week: $5, $10, $15, $20… grows by 5 each time.
2Pattern rules and predictionsStart at… then add…›
I can write a pattern rule and use it to predict. Patterns 2
A pattern rule says where to start and what to do each time: "Start at 4, add 3." A table helps you see it.

Example Saving for a bike

You have $20 and save $15 each week.
Week01234

Rule: start at 20, add 15. Week 10: 20 + 15 × 10 = $170.

3VariablesA letter for the unknown›
I can use a variable to describe an everyday situation. Patterns 3
A variable is a letter that stands for a number that can change or that we don't know yet.

Examples

  • You earn $16 an hour for h hours: pay = 16h
  • A taxi charges $4 plus $2 per km for k km: cost = 4 + 2k
  • Tickets cost $12 each for n people: total = 12n. For 5 people: 12 × 5 = $60
4Keeping it balancedEquations are like a scale›
I can show that an equation stays balanced. Patterns 4
An equation is like a balance scale: both sides are equal. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other and it stays balanced.

Example

x + 3 = 8. Take 3 away from both sides: x + 3 − 3 = 8 − 3, so x = 5. Check: 5 + 3 = 8 ✓
5Solving equationsFind the unknown›
I can solve equations with one unknown. Patterns 5
Do the opposite operation to get x alone: + ↔ − and × ↔ ÷. Then check by putting your answer back in.

Example 1

x − 6 = 9 → add 6 to both sides → x = 15

Example 2 On the job

You earned $96 at $16 an hour. How many hours? 16h = 96 → divide both sides by 16 → h = 6 hours.
