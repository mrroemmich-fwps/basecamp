Unit 5

Persuasion & PSAs

Want a later bedtime, a new school club or people to stop littering? You'll need to persuade. In this unit you'll pick an audience and purpose, write a persuasive paragraph with strong reasons, design a public service announcement (PSA), and present it with confidence.

2Lessons

1Audience and purposeWho am I talking to, and why?›
I can identify the audience and purpose of a message. 1.2.3, 3.1.1
The audience is who will read or hear it. The purpose is what you want to happen. The same request sounds very different to a friend, a parent or a principal.

Example Asking for a skate park

To friends: "Let's get a skate park! Sign the petition."
To town council: "A skate park would give 200 local youth a safe place to be active after school."
Same goal, different words for each audience.
2The persuasive paragraphPosition, reasons, call to action›
I can write a persuasive paragraph with a clear position and reasons. 2.4.3
Position (what you believe) → 3 reasons, each with an example or fact → counter-argument (what the other side might say, and your answer) → call to action (what you want the reader to do).

Example

Our school should have a recycling program in every classroom. First, it would keep hundreds of bottles out of the landfill each month. Second, the bottle deposits could raise money for field trips. Third, it teaches responsibility. Some people say it would be too much work, but student volunteers could collect bins once a week. Let's start a recycling team this month.
3Revise and editMake it stronger, make it right›
I can revise and edit with feedback. 4.1.1, 4.1.2
Revising makes your ideas stronger (add a reason, cut what's off-topic, pick stronger words). Editing fixes mistakes (capitals, punctuation, spelling, complete sentences). Do revising first, then editing.

CUPS checklist

  • Capitals: sentences, names, places
  • Usage: words make sense, verbs match
  • Punctuation: periods, commas, question marks
  • Spelling: check tricky words
4Designing a PSAWords + visuals›
I can design a PSA using words and visuals. 2.4.2, 4.3.2
A public service announcement gives the public an important message, like staying safe on ice or not texting and driving. A strong PSA has a short slogan, one clear image, a fact and a call to action. Keep text big and simple.

Example A poster

Image: a phone on a dashboard. Slogan: "That text can wait." Fact: distracted driving is a leading cause of crashes. Action: "Put your phone in the back seat."
5Presenting with confidenceVoice and body language›
I can present to an audience with voice and body language. 4.3.1, 4.3.3
Your voice: loud enough for the back row, not too fast, with pauses. Your body: stand tall, face the audience, look up from your notes, use your hands to point to visuals. Practise out loud at least 3 times.

Persuasion & PSAs Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Language Arts

Audience: who it's for. Purpose: what you want to happen.

Persuasive paragraph: position → 3 reasons with examples → counter-argument and answer → call to action.

Revise (better ideas) first, then edit with CUPS: Capitals · Usage · Punctuation · Spelling.

PSA: short slogan · one strong image · one fact · call to action · big, simple text.

Presenting: loud and slow · pause · stand tall · eye contact · practise 3 times.
