Unit 6

Time & Wages

At a job, time is money. In this unit you'll read 12-hour and 24-hour clocks, convert hours, minutes and seconds, figure out how long a shift was from a timecard, and calculate what you earned.

212-hour and 24-hour clocksa.m., p.m. and 14:30›
I can read and convert times on 12-hour and 24-hour clocks. Shape & Space 11
A 24-hour clock doesn't use a.m. or p.m. After noon, add 12 to the hour. To go back, subtract 12. Hospitals, airports, transit and many workplaces use 24-hour time.

Example

Your bus leaves at 17:20. 17 − 12 = 5, so 5:20 p.m.
3Converting hours, minutes and seconds× 60 and ÷ 60›
I can convert between hours, minutes and seconds. Shape & Space 12
Going to a smaller unit: × 60. Going to a bigger unit: ÷ 60.

Example 1

2.5 hours → 2.5 × 60 = 150 minutes

Example 2 Minutes to hours on a timecard

Payroll uses decimal hours. 45 minutes ÷ 60 = 0.75 hours. 15 min = 0.25 h, 30 min = 0.5 h, 45 min = 0.75 h.
4Elapsed time and timecardsHow long was the shift?›
I can find elapsed time and fill in a timecard. Shape & Space 11
Elapsed time is how much time passes. Count up from the start time: to the next hour, then whole hours, then the minutes left. Subtract unpaid breaks.

5Earning wagesHours × rate›
I can calculate wages from hours worked. Number 10
pay = hours worked × hourly rate. Use decimal hours (6 h 30 min = 6.5 h).

Example 1 The timecard above

14.25 hours × $16.50 = $235.13 (235.125 rounds up). This is gross pay, before deductions.

Example 2 Overtime

In Alberta, most workers earn overtime (1.5 × their rate) for hours over 8 in a day or over 44 in a week. If you earn $16 an hour, overtime is 1.5 × 16 = $24 an hour.

Time & Wages Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Math

60 s = 1 min · 60 min = 1 h · 24 h = 1 day · 7 days = 1 week · 52 weeks ≈ 1 year · 100 years = 1 century · 1,000 years = 1 millennium

24-hour clock: after noon, add 12 (2:30 p.m. = 14:30). To go back, subtract 12 (17:20 = 5:20 p.m.). Midnight = 00:00.

Convert: to a smaller unit × 60 · to a bigger unit ÷ 60. 15 min = 0.25 h · 30 min = 0.5 h · 45 min = 0.75 h

Elapsed time: count up to the next hour, then whole hours, then the minutes left. Subtract unpaid breaks.

Pay = hours × hourly rate (gross pay). Alberta overtime: 1.5 × rate over 8 h a day or 44 h a week.
