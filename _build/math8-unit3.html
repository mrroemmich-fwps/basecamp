Unit 3

Decimals & Money

Every price, paycheque and receipt uses decimals. In this unit you'll read tenths and hundredths, compare prices, add up a bill, make change like a cashier, and split costs fairly. It's the math behind the class store!

1Tenths and hundredthsDimes and pennies›
I can explain tenths and hundredths using money. Number 3
After the decimal point: the first place is tenths (dimes, 10¢) and the second is hundredths (cents, 1¢).
Dollars.Tenths (dimes)Hundredths (cents)
4.75

Example $4.75

4 dollars, 7 dimes and 5 cents. $4.75 = 4 + 0.7 + 0.05. Canada stopped making pennies, so cash totals round to the nearest 5¢, but cards and receipts still use every cent.
2Comparing decimalsWhich costs more?›
I can compare and order decimals. Number 6
Line up the decimal points, add zeros so they have the same number of places, then compare from left to right.

Example 0.5 or 0.45?

  1. Write 0.5 as 0.50.
  2. Compare 0.50 and 0.45: tenths 5 > 4.
  3. 0.5 is greater, even though 45 looks bigger than 5!
3Adding and subtracting moneyReceipts and budgets›
I can add and subtract money amounts. Number 7, 10
Line up the decimal points before you add or subtract, just like on a receipt.

Example A snack run

Chips $ 3.49 Juice $ 2.25 + Granola $ 4.80 ─────────────── Total $10.54

You have $15.00: $15.00 − $10.54 = $4.46 left.

4Making changeCount up like a cashier›
I can count and make change up to $100. Number 10
Cashiers count up from the price to the amount paid: first to the next quarter or dollar, then to the next 5, 10 or 20.

Example $7.35 paid with a $20 bill

  1. $7.35 + 0.65 (2 quarters, 1 dime, 1 nickel) → $8.00
  2. $8.00 + 2.00 (a toonie) → $10.00
  3. $10.00 + 10.00 (a $10 bill) → $20.00
  4. Change: $0.65 + $2.00 + $10.00 = $12.65
🏔️ Avalanche HQ connection: When you buy something in the class store, the app does this math for you. Check it yourself!
5Multiplying and dividing moneyBuying several, splitting the bill›
I can multiply and divide money to buy several items or split a bill. Number 7, 10
Buying several: price × how many. Splitting: total ÷ number of people. Round money to the nearest cent.

Example 1 Buying several

4 slices of pizza at $3.75 each: 4 × 3.75 = $15.00. Check: $3.75 is almost $4, and 4 × 4 = 16. Close!

Example 2 Splitting a bill

3 friends split a $45 order: 45 ÷ 3 = $15 each.
