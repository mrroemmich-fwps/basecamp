Unit 9

Mass, Volume & Capacity

How heavy is it? How much does it hold? Cooks, mechanics, pharmacists and shippers need to know. In this unit you'll measure mass and capacity in metric and imperial, connect cubic centimetres to millilitres, and build 3-D objects from nets.

1MassGrams, kilograms and pounds›
I can measure and convert mass in grams, kilograms and pounds. Shape & Space 7
Mass is how heavy something is. 1,000 g = 1 kg. In imperial, 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb.
ObjectAbout
A paper clip1 g
A loaf of bread675 g
A litre of milkabout 1 kg
A bag of flour10 kg ≈ 22 lb

Example

A recipe needs 1.5 kg of potatoes. 1.5 × 1,000 = 1,500 g. In pounds: 1.5 × 2.2 = 3.3 lb.
2CapacityMillilitres, litres and cups›
I can measure and convert capacity in millilitres, litres and cups. Shape & Space 7
Capacity is how much a container holds. 1,000 mL = 1 L. Canadian measuring cups hold about 250 mL.

Example 1

A 2 L jug of juice fills how many 250 mL glasses? 2,000 ÷ 250 = 8 glasses.

Example 2 On the job

A mechanic needs 4.5 L of oil. Oil comes in 946 mL bottles (1 U.S. quart). 4,500 ÷ 946 ≈ 4.8, so buy 5 bottles.
3Volume: cm³ and mLA cube that holds 1 mL›
I can show that 1 cm³ holds 1 mL. Shape & Space 8
Volume of a box = length × width × height, in cubic centimetres (cm³). A 1 cm cube holds exactly 1 mL of water, so cm³ = mL.

Example A fish tank

A tank is 50 cm × 25 cm × 30 cm. 50 × 25 × 30 = 37,500 cm³ = 37,500 mL = 37.5 L of water.

Capacity: 1,000 mL = 1 L · 1 Canadian cup ≈ 250 mL

Volume of a box: l × w × h (cm³). 1 cm³ = 1 mL, so 1,000 cm³ = 1 L.

Converting metric: bigger unit → smaller unit: × 1,000. Smaller → bigger: ÷ 1,000.
