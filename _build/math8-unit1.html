Unit 1

Number Sense

Big numbers show up everywhere: house prices, populations, paycheques, kilometres on a truck. In this unit you'll read, write, compare and round numbers up to a million, and learn to tell when an answer makes sense.

1Place value to a millionEvery digit has a job›
I can explain the value of each digit in a number up to 1,000,000. Number 1, 3
A digit's place tells you its value. Each place is worth 10 times the place to its right.

2Reading and writing big numbersNumerals and words›
I can read and write big numbers in numerals and in words. Number 1
Read big numbers in groups of three, separated by commas or spaces. Say the group, then its name: thousand, then the rest.

Example 1 Numerals to words

305,040 → three hundred five thousand forty

Example 2 Words to numerals

"Sixty-two thousand eight" → 62 thousand and 8 ones → 62,008. The zeros hold the empty places.

Example 3 On a cheque

Cheques and some forms ask for the amount in words so no one can change it: $1,250 is written one thousand two hundred fifty dollars.
3Comparing and orderingWhich is bigger?›
I can compare and order whole numbers. Number 2
Compare numbers from left to right. First check how many digits; then compare the biggest place first. Use > (greater than) and < (less than).

Example Three trucks for sale

Kilometres driven: 84,500 · 48,900 · 84,050
  1. All have 5 digits. Compare ten thousands: 8, 4, 8. So 48,900 is smallest.
  2. 84,500 vs 84,050: compare hundreds: 5 > 0.
  3. Least to greatest: 48,900 < 84,050 < 84,500
4Rounding and estimatingClose enough to decide fast›
I can round and estimate to make quick decisions. Number 1, 12
To round, look at the digit just to the right of the place you're rounding to. 5 or more → round up. 4 or less → stay.

Example 1 Round 47,382 to the nearest thousand

  1. Thousands digit: 7. Digit to the right: 3.
  2. 3 is 4 or less, so stay: 47,000

Example 2 Estimating a total

A family buys a fridge for $1,189, a stove for $879 and a microwave for $219. Round to the nearest hundred: 1,200 + 900 + 200 = about $2,300.
5Does it make sense?Estimate before you calculate›
I can check whether a calculator answer makes sense. Number 12
Calculators don't make mistakes, but people press the wrong buttons. Estimate first, then compare.

Example A missed digit

Jordan adds 4,890 + 2,105 and the calculator shows 2,594.
  1. Estimate: 5,000 + 2,000 = about 7,000.
  2. 2,594 is way off. Jordan typed 489 instead of 4,890.
  3. Real answer: 6,995, close to the estimate.
