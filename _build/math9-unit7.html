Unit 7

Patterns & Equations

A taxi fare, a phone plan, a plumber's bill: many costs follow a rule, like a starting fee plus a rate. In this unit you'll describe patterns, write expressions for them, solve two-step equations, and read graphs of real relationships.

1Patterns and predictionsWhat comes next?›
I can describe and extend patterns, and make predictions. Patterns 1, 2
Find what changes each step (the rate) and where it starts. Then you can predict any step without listing them all.

Example Saving up

You have $35 and save $20 every week: 35, 55, 75, 95… Start at 35, add 20.
After 12 weeks: 35 + 20 × 12 = $275.
2Expressions and rulesStart fee + rate × amount›
I can write an expression or rule for a pattern. Patterns 3
Many costs have a fixed part and a part that changes. Use a letter (a variable) for the amount that changes. cost = fixed fee + rate × n

Example A phone plan

A plan costs $25 a month plus $3 for every GB of data. Rule: C = 25 + 3g.
Using 6 GB: C = 25 + 3 × 6 = 25 + 18 = $43.
3Two-step equationsUndo in reverse order›
I can solve two-step equations by keeping them balanced. Patterns 4
To get x alone, undo the adding or subtracting first, then undo the multiplying. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other.

Example 1

3x + 4 = 19
Subtract 4 from both sides: 3x = 15
Divide both sides by 3: x = 5. Check: 3 × 5 + 4 = 19 ✓

Example 2 Working backwards

A plumber charges $60 plus $45 an hour. The bill was $195. How many hours?
60 + 45h = 195 → 45h = 135 → h = 3 hours.
4Tables and graphsPicture a relationship›
I can make a table of values and graph a relationship. Patterns 5
Use the rule to fill a table of values. Each row becomes a point on a graph: the input goes across (x) and the output goes up (y). A steady rate makes a straight line.

Example Earning at $15 an hour

Hours (x)01234
Pay $ (y)015304560
Each step right (1 hour), the line goes up $15. That's the rate.
5Reading graphsWhat does the line tell you?›
I can read a graph and draw conclusions. Patterns 2, 5
Read the title and both axes first. Where lines cross, the two options are equal. Before that point one is cheaper, after it the other is.

Example Two gyms

Gym A: $40 to join + $5 a visit. Gym B: no fee, $10 a visit. The lines cross at 8 visits. Fewer than 8 visits: Gym B is cheaper. More than 8: Gym A is cheaper.

Patterns & Equations Cheat Sheet · Grade 9 Math

Pattern rule: where it starts + how much it changes each step. Prediction = start + rate × steps.

Expression: fixed fee + rate × n. A $25 plan plus $3 per GB: C = 25 + 3g.

Solve 3x + 4 = 19
1. Undo + or − (subtract 4 from both sides)3x = 15
2. Undo × or ÷ (divide both sides by 3)x = 5
3. Check: put it back in3 × 5 + 4 = 19 ✓

Graphs: input goes across (x), output goes up (y). A steady rate makes a straight line. A bigger rate makes a steeper line.

Where lines cross: both options are equal. Check which line is lower on each side to find the cheaper choice.
