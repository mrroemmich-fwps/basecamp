Unit 1

Identity & Belonging

Who are you, and where do you belong? In this unit you'll read and listen to stories about identity, write about your own life, set goals for yourself as a reader and writer, and practise being a great member of a group.

2Lessons

1What shapes who I am?Identity maps›
I can connect stories to my own life and experiences. 1.1.1, 2.2.1
Your identity is made of many parts: family, culture, language, interests, places, beliefs and experiences. When you read, look for connections: text-to-self ("That happened to me"), text-to-text ("That's like another story") and text-to-world ("That happens in real life").

Example An identity map

Put your name in the centre. Around it, add branches: family (oldest of 3), places (grew up on a farm near Ponoka), interests (hockey, drawing), language (English and Cree), values (loyalty). Each branch is a story you could tell.
2Telling my storyThe personal narrative paragraph›
I can write a personal narrative paragraph. 2.4.1, 2.4.3
A narrative paragraph tells one true moment from your life. It has a topic sentence that hooks the reader, details in order (what you saw, heard, felt), and a closing sentence that says why it mattered.

Example

The day I finally landed a kickflip, I learned that falling is part of getting better. For three weeks I practised in the Co-op parking lot after school. My shins were covered in bruises and my little brother laughed every time I wiped out. Then, on a cold Thursday, the board spun, my feet caught it, and I rolled away clean. Now, when something is hard, I remember those bruises and keep going.
3Stories and cultureOral stories and belonging›
I can explain how stories show culture and identity, including First Nations, Métis and Inuit stories. 5.1.1, 5.1.2
Every culture passes on what it values through stories. Many First Nations, Métis and Inuit stories are oral: told and retold by Elders and Knowledge Keepers. They often teach lessons about respect, the land and how to live together. Some stories are only told at certain times or by certain people, so we treat them with respect.

How to respond to a story

  • Who is telling it, and to whom?
  • What does it teach about values (respect, sharing, courage)?
  • What does it show about the land or community?
  • How does it connect to your life?
4Me as a learnerStrengths and goals›
I can set reading and writing goals for myself. 1.1.4
Good learners know their strengths and their stretch areas. A SMART goal is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound.

Example

Not SMART: "Read more."
SMART: "I will read for 15 minutes, 4 nights a week, until the winter break, and log it in my planner."
5Belonging in a groupRespectful teamwork›
I can work respectfully in a group. 5.1.4, 5.2.1
Everyone does better when they feel they belong. In a group, use inclusive language, take turns, build on others' ideas, and give constructive feedback (helpful), not ridicule.
