Unit 5

Planning My Future

The courses you pick in high school can open or close doors. In this unit you'll compare post-secondary pathways, learn about admission requirements, use labour market information to check your goals, compare costs and ways to pay for school, and start a high school course plan.

1Pathways after high schoolMany roads, all valuable›
I can compare post-secondary pathways and their admission requirements. 8.2
PathwayLengthExample
ApprenticeshipUsually 2–4 years, mostly paid on-the-jobElectrician, cook, welder
College or polytechnic certificate/diplomaA few months to 2 yearsHealth-care aide, vet tech, business admin
University degree4+ yearsTeacher, engineer, nurse

Admission requirements are the courses and marks you need to get in. Some programs are competitive: meeting the minimum doesn't guarantee a spot.
2Reality checkLabour market information›
I can use labour market information to check a career goal. 8.2
Labour market information (LMI) shows which careers are in demand now and may be in the future, what they pay and where the jobs are. Compare your dream with the data: Is it growing? What does it pay? Do you need to move?
3Paying for schoolTuition and free money›
I can compare tuition and identify grants, bursaries and scholarships. 8.2
Tuition varies a lot between programs. Add books, fees, rent and food for the full cost. Money you don't pay back: scholarships (usually for marks or achievement), bursaries (usually for financial need) and grants (often government, based on need). Student loans must be paid back.
4NetworkingPeople open doors›
I can build my network and start conversations about careers. 8.2
Networking is building relationships to share information and support. Your network includes family, neighbours, coaches, teachers, employers and volunteers you've worked with. Many jobs are found through people you know.

Conversation starters

  • "How did you get into your job?"
  • "What does a normal day look like?"
  • "What do you wish you'd known at my age?"
  • "Is there someone else I could talk to?"
5My course planKeep doors open›
I can draft a high school course plan that keeps options open. 8.2
Look at what your goal pathway requires, then work backwards to Grade 10. Early planning keeps more options available. Talk to your school counsellor about pathways, RAP and work experience.

Planning My Future Cheat Sheet · CEFL 8

Pathways: apprenticeship · college/polytechnic · university · straight to work. All are valuable.

Admission requirements: courses and marks needed. Competitive = minimum isn't a guarantee.

LMI: demand, wages and locations for careers. Check ALIS OCCinfo.

Free money: scholarships (achievement) · bursaries (need) · grants. Loans must be repaid.

Networking: build relationships, ask good questions, say thank you.
