Unit 1

Worldviews & Identity

Everyone sees the world through a lens shaped by family, culture, beliefs and experience. That lens is called a worldview. In this unit you'll explore your own worldview, learn the tools historians use, and get ready to see how different societies met, clashed and changed.

1My worldviewThe lens I see through›
I can explain what a worldview is and describe parts of my own. 8 overview · I
A worldview is the set of beliefs, values and knowledge that shape how a person or society understands the world. It answers big questions: What matters most? How should people treat each other and the land? Where do we come from?

What shapes a worldview?

  • Family and culture
  • Religion or spirituality
  • Where and when you live (geography and time)
  • Language
  • Experiences and education
2PerspectivesSame event, different views›
I can recognize that people have different perspectives. Skills
A perspective is a point of view on a specific event or issue. People with different worldviews often see the same event very differently. Historians try to hear all sides.

Example

When a new mine opens near a town: the mine owner sees jobs and profit; a local family sees paycheques but worries about water; a First Nation nearby may see harm to traditional hunting lands. One event, several valid perspectives.
3Thinking like a historianSources and bias›
I can tell primary sources from secondary sources and check for bias. Skills
A primary source was made at the time by someone who was there (a diary, photo, artifact, oral history). A secondary source was made later by someone studying it (a textbook, documentary). Every source has a creator with a perspective, so ask who made it and why.
4Timelines and mapsWhen and where›
I can place events on a timeline and read historical maps. Skills · TCC, LPP
A timeline shows events in order. CE (Common Era) years count up from year 1; BCE years count down to it. A century is 100 years: the 1500s are the 16th century. Historical maps show places, routes and borders at a certain time.
5Worldviews and the landFirst Nations, Métis and Inuit perspectives›
I can describe how First Nations, Métis and Inuit worldviews connect to the land. 8.1
Many First Nations, Métis and Inuit worldviews see people as part of the natural world, not above it. Common teachings include respect, reciprocity (giving back), interconnection and responsibility to future generations. Each Nation has its own teachings, languages and ways of expressing them.

Worldviews & Identity Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Social Studies

Worldview: the beliefs, values and knowledge that shape how a person or society sees the world. Shaped by family, culture, spirituality, place, time, language and experience.

Perspective: a point of view on a specific event or issue.

Primary source: made at the time by someone there (diary, photo, artifact, oral history). Secondary: made later (textbook, documentary).

Centuries: the 1500s = 16th century. CE counts up, BCE counts down.

Key words: respect · reciprocity · interconnection · future generations.
