2Lessons

1Formal or informal?Match your words to your reader›
I can choose formal or informal language to fit who I'm writing to. 4.2.1, 4.1.4
Informal language is for friends and family. Formal language is for bosses, teachers, customers and anyone you don't know well. Ask: Who is reading this?
Informal (to a friend)Formal (to a boss)
hey whats upHello Ms. Patel,
cant make it 2day 😬I'm sorry, but I won't be able to work my shift today.
idkI'm not sure. I will find out.
thx!!Thank you for your help.

Example Same message, two readers

To a friend: "Running late lol, see u at 4."
To a boss: "Hi Mr. Chen, I'm running about 15 minutes late because my bus was delayed. I'll be there by 4:15. Sorry for the trouble."
The formal version uses full words, no slang, a greeting and a reason.
2Writing a workplace emailFive parts every email needs›
I can write a clear workplace email. 2.4.3
A good work email has 5 parts: subject line, greeting, message, closing, name. Keep it short and get to the point.

Example Asking for a day off

Subject: Day off request for Friday, Dec. 4 Hello Ms. Patel, I would like to ask for Friday, December 4 off. I have a family event that day. I can work Saturday instead if that helps. Thank you, Jordan Lee
  1. Subject: says what it's about, with the date.
  2. Greeting: "Hello" + the person's name.
  3. Message: what you want, why, and an offer to help.
  4. Closing: "Thank you," or "Sincerely,"
  5. Name: your full name.
3Personal lettersThank-you notes and more›
I can write a personal letter, like a thank-you letter. 2.4.3
A personal letter has a date, greeting, body, closing and signature. A good thank-you letter says what you're thankful for and why it mattered.

Example A thank-you letter

November 20, 2026 Dear Mr. Okafor, Thank you for letting our class tour your auto shop. I learned how an oil change works and why safety glasses matter. Seeing the tools up close made me think about becoming a mechanic. Sincerely, Riley
4Filling out formsJob applications and other forms›
I can fill out work and home forms neatly and correctly. 2.4.3, 2.1.2
Forms are often the first thing an employer sees. Read the whole form first, print neatly, and don't leave blanks.

Form tips

  • Read every instruction first. Some say "print in black ink" or "use CAPITAL LETTERS."
  • Write N/A (not applicable) if a question doesn't apply to you. Don't leave it blank.
  • Dates are often written YYYY-MM-DD (2026-11-20).
  • Keep private information private. Only put your SIN (Social Insurance Number) on a form from your actual employer, never on a practice form or website you don't trust.
  • Check spelling, then sign and date at the end.

5Following instructionsRead, listen, then say it back›
I can follow and paraphrase written and spoken instructions. 2.1.2, 4.3.4
To paraphrase is to say something again in your own words. Saying instructions back is the best way to check you understood them.

Example At a café job

Your supervisor says: "Before you leave, wipe the counters, restock the cups and lids, and lock the back door. Then text me."
  1. Paraphrase: "So: counters, cups and lids, lock the back door, then text you. Got it."
  2. Make a quick numbered list if there are more than 3 steps.
  3. If something isn't clear, ask before you start.
6Proofread before you sendCapitals, punctuation and spelling›
I can proofread for capitals, punctuation and spelling. 4.1.2, 4.2.2, 4.2.3
One small mistake can make a good email look careless. Read it slowly, out loud, once for each thing on the checklist.

Proofreading checklist

  • Capitals: first word of each sentence, names, days and months, the word "I."
  • Punctuation: a period, question mark or exclamation mark at the end of each sentence.
  • Spelling: watch for there/their/they're, your/you're, to/too/two.
  • Make sense: read it out loud. Does it sound right?

Example Fix it

✗ "i can work monday if your ok with that"
✓ "I can work Monday if you're OK with that."

  • Fill out a real form together, like a library card or sports registration.
  • Give 3-step instructions and ask them to say them back before starting.
