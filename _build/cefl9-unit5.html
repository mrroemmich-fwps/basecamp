Unit 5

Planning My Future

Is more school worth it? How will you pay for it? What if plan A doesn't work out? In this unit you'll figure out the true cost of a pathway, compare its return on investment, learn how student loans and financial aid work, explore earn-while-you-learn options, and build a post-secondary plan with a backup.

1The true costMore than tuition›
I can calculate the true cost of a post-secondary pathway. 9.2
The true cost = tuition + fees + books + living costs (rent, food, transportation) + opportunity cost: the money you could have earned working instead of being in school.

Example A made-up 2-year diploma

Tuition and fees $8,000/yr × 2 = $16,000. Books $1,500. Living at home, transportation $3,000. Opportunity cost: a $25,000/yr job × 2 = $50,000. True cost ≈ $70,500.
2Is it worth it?Return on investment›
I can compare the return on investment of different pathways. 9.2
Return on investment (ROI) compares the total cost of a pathway with the expected income and opportunities over time. It depends on graduation rates, how many grads find jobs in that field, and the job market where you'll live. Personal values (enjoying your work, location, family) matter too.
3Student loans and aidBorrow wisely›
I can explain student loans and financial aid, including repayment. 9.2
Student loans are debt that must be repaid after you finish school, and some may charge interest, so check the current rules before you borrow. Grants, bursaries and scholarships don't need to be paid back. Borrow only what you need, and know your monthly payment before you sign.
4Earn while you learnCo-op, internships and apprenticeships›
I can explain how work-integrated learning and other factors shape a pathway. 9.2
Work-integrated learning (co-op, internships, apprenticeships) lets you earn money and build experience during your training, lowering costs and improving job chances. Competitive programs may also look at portfolios, interviews, references and extracurriculars. If you move, your credentials may not be recognized everywhere. And it's never too late: adults change careers and return to school all the time.
5My post-secondary planPlan A, plan B, and why›
I can build a post-secondary plan with a primary pathway and a backup. 9.2
A strong plan has a primary pathway, a contingency (backup) and a rationale that covers money, personal fit and labour market data.

Example

Plan A: Registered Apprenticeship Program in Grade 11, then a heavy equipment technician apprenticeship. Plan B: parts technician certificate. Why: earn while learning, strong demand in Alberta, I love fixing engines. Money, fit and data all point the same way.

Planning My Future Cheat Sheet · CEFL 9

True cost = tuition + fees + books + living costs + opportunity cost (wages given up).

ROI: total cost vs. expected income and opportunities. Depends on graduation and employment rates and the regional job market.

Free money first: scholarships · bursaries · grants. Loans are debt, repaid after school.

Work-integrated learning: co-op · internships · apprenticeships. Earn while you learn.

Plan: primary pathway + contingency + rationale (money, fit, data).
