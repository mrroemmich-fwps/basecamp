Unit 8

Perimeter, Area & Angles

Building a deck, laying sod, framing a roof, drawing a floor plan: the trades are full of shapes and angles. In this unit you'll find the area of rectangles, triangles and parallelograms, design rectangles to fit a space, measure and classify angles, and use scale drawings.

1Designing rectanglesSame perimeter, different area›
I can find area and design rectangles for a given perimeter or area. Shape & Space 5, 7
Area = length × width, in square units. Rectangles with the same perimeter can have very different areas. The closer to a square, the more area you get.

Example 24 m of fence for a garden

LengthWidthPerimeterArea
10 m2 m24 m20 m²
8 m4 m24 m32 m²
6 m6 m24 m36 m² (the most)
2Triangles and parallelogramsBase × height›
I can find the area of triangles and parallelograms. Shape & Space 6
A parallelogram is a rectangle that's been pushed over: A = base × height. A triangle is half a parallelogram: A = base × height ÷ 2. The height is always measured straight up (at 90°) from the base.

Example A gable wall

The triangle at the end of a shed roof has a base of 3 m and a height of 1.2 m. A = 3 × 1.2 ÷ 2 = 1.8 m² of siding.

To use a protractor: put the centre hole on the vertex (the point), line up one arm with 0°, then read where the other arm crosses. Estimate first so you read the right scale.
4TrianglesAngles add to 180°›
I can classify triangles by their angles and sides. Shape & Space 16
The three angles in any triangle add up to 180°. By angles: acute (all under 90°), right (one 90°), obtuse (one over 90°). By sides: equilateral (all equal), isosceles (two equal), scalene (none equal).

Example A roof truss

A truss has angles of 30° and 30°. The third angle is 180 − 30 − 30 = 120°. It's an obtuse isosceles triangle.
5Scale and gridsPlans, maps and coordinates›
I can use a scale to draw or read a plan, and plot points in two quadrants. Shape & Space 17, 19, 20
A scale like 1 cm = 2 m shrinks real things to fit on paper. Multiply to go from the plan to real life, divide to go from real life to the plan. On a grid, (x, y) means across, then up. A negative x means go left of zero (the second quadrant).

Example 1 A floor plan

A plan uses 1 cm = 50 cm. A 4 m (400 cm) wall is drawn 400 ÷ 50 = 8 cm long.

Example 2 Directions

Start at (0, 0). Go 3 left and 4 up. You're at (−3, 4).

Perimeter, Area & Angles Cheat Sheet · Grade 9 Math

ShapeArea
Rectanglelength × width
Parallelogrambase × height
Trianglebase × height ÷ 2

Height is always measured at 90° to the base. Same perimeter? The squarest rectangle has the most area.

Angles: acute < 90° · right = 90° · obtuse 90°–180° · straight = 180° · reflex > 180°

Triangles: angles add to 180°. By sides: equilateral (3 equal), isosceles (2 equal), scalene (none).

Scale: plan → real: multiply. Real → plan: divide. Use the same units.

Grid: (x, y) = across, then up. Negative x = left of zero (second quadrant).
