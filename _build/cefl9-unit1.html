Unit 1

Skills, Identity & the World of Work

Robots, apps and global competition are changing which jobs exist. In this unit you'll look at how automation and globalization reshape work, sort industries into growing, stable and declining, find reliable labour market data, and see how volunteering, entrepreneurship and language skills can open new paths.

1AutomationMachines doing human tasks›
I can analyze how automation and globalization are reshaping jobs. 9.1
Automation uses technology (robots, software, AI) to do tasks people used to do. It can disrupt jobs, especially repetitive ones, but it also creates jobs (installing, fixing, programming and supervising machines). Globalization connects labour markets across borders, so you may compete with, or work with, people around the world.
Less likely to be automatedMore likely
Jobs needing hands-on skill in changing places (electricians, plumbers)Repetitive data entry
Caring and people skills (nurses, early childhood educators)Simple assembly-line tasks
2Where's the work going?Growing, stable, declining›
I can identify growing, stable and declining industries using data. 9.1
Industries can be growing (more jobs each year), stable or declining. Clues: labour market trends, government policy (new rules or funding), investment (companies building new plants) and technology. Career success depends on where the market is going, not just where it is.
3Reliable LMIWhere to find the facts›
I can find labour market information from reliable sources. 9.1
Good labour market information comes from government databases (ALIS, Job Bank, Statistics Canada), industry reports and professional associations. Be careful with social media posts about "jobs that pay $100K with no training."
4Learning by doingVolunteering and entrepreneurship›
I can explain how volunteering and entrepreneurship build skills and career interest. 9.3
Volunteering lets you try different kinds of work and places. Entrepreneurship (starting your own business) teaches you how businesses run: a business plan, managing money, taking risks and sometimes managing people. Skills grow through formal experiences (courses, training) and informal ones (learning from family).
🏔️ Avalanche HQ connection: Pitch a mini-business idea for the class store, with a simple business plan.
5Language as a skillBilingual careers›
I can identify careers where bilingualism is an asset. 9.3
Some Canadian careers require English and French (many federal government jobs). In others, any second language is an asset: health care, tourism, customer service, law enforcement and trades serving newcomers. Languages can be learned formally (classes) and informally (family, community).
