Unit 3

Mechanical Systems

Bikes, wrenches, cranes, can openers and even your own arm are machines. In this unit you'll see how simple machines make work easier, how gears and linkages pass force along, and how to judge whether a machine does its job well.

1Machines over timeFrom muscle to motors›
I can describe how machines have changed over time. D1
People have always needed to lift, move and cut things. The need stays the same, but the machines and their energy sources change: muscles → animals → water and wind → engines → electricity and batteries.

2Simple machinesSix ways to make work easier›
I can identify simple machines in everyday and traditional tools. D2
There are six simple machines. Most tools are one of these, or several put together (a compound machine).

Example A traditional travois

A travois is two long poles with a platform between them. One end rests on a dog or horse and the other drags on the ground, so the poles work like levers. The animal can pull a load much heavier than it could carry.
3Levers and mechanical advantageLift more with less›
I can explain levers and find mechanical advantage. D2, D3
A lever has a fulcrum (the pivot), a load (what you move) and an effort (your push). Mechanical advantage (MA) tells how many times a machine multiplies your force: MA = load ÷ effort. Moving the fulcrum closer to the load makes lifting easier.

Example A crowbar

You push with 50 N and lift a 400 N crate. MA = 400 ÷ 50 = 8. The crowbar makes your push 8 times stronger, but you have to push your end much farther.
4Gears, linkages and workPassing force along›
I can explain how gears and linkages transfer force, and measure work in joules. D2, D3
Gears pass turning force from one shaft to another. A small gear turning a big gear gives more force but less speed. A big gear turning a small gear gives more speed but less force. Work is force moving something a distance: work (J) = force (N) × distance (m).

Example 1 Bike gears

Climbing a hill, you shift so the chain is on a small front gear and a big back gear. Pedalling is easier, but you go slower. On flat ground, you shift the other way for more speed.

Example 2 Work

You push a wheelbarrow with 120 N for 5 m. Work = 120 × 5 = 600 J.
5Judging a machineDoes it do the job well?›
I can evaluate a machine for function, safety and efficiency. D4
Engineers judge a design with criteria: Does it work? Is it reliable? Is it safe? Is it efficient (little energy wasted)? What's its effect on the environment? What does it cost?

Example Snow removal

The "best" choice depends on which criteria matter most for the size of the driveway and the person using it.

Mechanical Systems Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Science

Six simple machines: lever · wheel and axle · pulley · inclined plane · wedge · screw. A compound machine combines several.

Lever parts: fulcrum (pivot) · load (what moves) · effort (your push). Fulcrum closer to the load = easier lift.

Mechanical advantage: MA = load ÷ effort. 400 N ÷ 50 N = 8.

Gears: small turning big = more force, less speed. Big turning small = more speed, less force.

Work: joules (J) = force (N) × distance (m). 120 N × 5 m = 600 J.

Judging a design: function · reliability · safety · efficiency · environment · cost.
