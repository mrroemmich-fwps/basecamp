Unit 10

Data & Probability

Businesses, coaches, doctors and governments all make decisions with data. In this unit you'll find good sources of data, read and build graphs, make predictions, and run probability experiments to see how chance really works.

2Lessons

1Where data comes fromFirst-hand and second-hand›
I can choose first-hand or second-hand data sources. Statistics 1
First-hand data is collected yourself (a survey, measuring, counting). Second-hand data was collected by someone else (Statistics Canada, a news article, a website).

Example

Want to know the class's favourite store item? Survey the class (first-hand). Want to know Alberta's population? Look it up from Statistics Canada (second-hand).
2Reading graphsWhat is this graph telling me?›
I can read and interpret graphs and charts. Statistics 2
Read the title, the labels on each axis and the scale first. Then look for the highest, the lowest and any trend.
3Organizing and displaying dataTallies, tables, graphs, spreadsheets›
I can collect, organize and display data. Statistics 5
Collect with a tally chart, organize in a frequency table, then pick a display: bar graph to compare groups, line graph to show change over time.

Example How do you get to school?

This compares groups, so a bar graph fits best. In a spreadsheet, type the table and use Insert → Chart.
4Predictions and conclusionsWhat does the data tell us?›
I can make predictions and conclusions from data. Statistics 3, 4
A conclusion says what the data shows. A prediction uses the data to guess what will happen next. Always ask: is there enough data to be sure?

Example Store sales

Chocolate sold the most in November. Conclusion: chocolate is the most popular item. Prediction: it will sell out first in December, so order more. But one month is a small sample; December might be different because of the holidays.
5Probability experimentsChance in action›
I can run probability experiments and explain the results. Statistics 6, 7
Probability is how likely something is. A coin has a 1 in 2 chance of heads. But in a real experiment, results vary. The more times you try, the closer you usually get to what's expected.

What to notice

Flip 10 times and you might get 7 heads. Flip 500 times and it's usually close to 50% each. Same experiment, different results, but more trials give a clearer picture.

Data & Probability Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Math

First-hand data: you collect it (survey, count, measure). Second-hand: someone else collected it. Check that the source is trustworthy and current.

Read a graph: title → axis labels → scale → highest, lowest, trend.

Display: tally chart → frequency table → bar graph (compare groups) or line graph (change over time).

Conclusion: what the data shows. Prediction: what will probably happen next. Is the sample big enough?

Probability: coin = 1 in 2 · die = 1 in 6 for each number. Small experiments vary; more trials get closer to what's expected.
