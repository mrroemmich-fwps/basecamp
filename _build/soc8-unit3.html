Unit 3

The Renaissance

About 600 years ago, cities in Italy started a "rebirth" of art, science and ideas that changed Europe and, eventually, the world. In this unit you'll find out what sparked the Renaissance, how new ideas spread, how trade and exploration grew, and how it all shaped the western worldview many Canadians live with today.

2Lessons

1A rebirthWhy Italy, why then?›
I can explain what the Renaissance was and why it started in Italy. 8.2 · TCC, LPP
Renaissance means "rebirth." From about the 1300s to the 1600s, Europeans rediscovered ancient Greek and Roman ideas. It began in Italian city-states like Florence and Venice, which were rich from trade across the Mediterranean. Wealthy families like the Medici paid artists and thinkers (they were patrons).
2HumanismPeople at the centre›
I can describe humanism and how it changed the way people saw the world. 8.2 · I
Humanism was a belief that individual people and their abilities matter, and that people can learn and improve through education. Art became more realistic (perspective, real human bodies). A "Renaissance person" is someone skilled in many areas, like Leonardo da Vinci: painter, inventor, engineer and scientist.
3Ideas spreadPrinting press, science and religion›
I can explain how the printing press spread new ideas in science, religion and art. 8.2 · GC
Around 1450, Johannes Gutenberg built a printing press with movable type. Books became cheaper and faster to make, so more people learned to read and ideas spread quickly. Copernicus argued the Earth orbits the Sun; Galileo used a telescope to support it. Martin Luther's printed ideas split the Church (the Reformation).

4Trade and explorationCompetition for routes and riches›
I can describe how geography, trade and competition led to exploration. 8.2 · ER, LPP
Europeans wanted spices, silk and gold from Asia, but land routes were long and controlled by others. Countries on the Atlantic, like Portugal and Spain, competed to find sea routes. New tools (the compass, better ships and maps) made it possible. In 1492, Columbus, sailing for Spain, reached the Americas.
5The western worldview todayRenaissance ideas in Canada›
I can connect Renaissance ideas to life in Canada today. 8.2 · C, I
Many ideas in Canada's dominant western worldview grew from the Renaissance: valuing the individual, science and evidence, public education, and trade and business. Seeing where these ideas came from helps us understand why other worldviews may see things differently.
