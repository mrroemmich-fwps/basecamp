Unit 5

Worldviews Today

Contact between worldviews didn't end centuries ago. It happens every day in Canada, online and around the world. In this unit you'll connect what you learned about Japan, the Renaissance and the Aztecs to today, analyze a current issue, and plan a way to make a difference.

2Keeping traditions, adapting to changeHere at home›
I can describe how First Nations, Métis and Inuit communities keep traditions while adapting. 8.1 · I, CC
Contact with European worldviews brought huge pressure on First Nations, Métis and Inuit languages, spirituality and relationships with the land, including through residential schools. Today, many communities are revitalizing languages and traditions while using modern tools, like language apps, Indigenous-run businesses and media, and land-based learning.
3Media and other culturesStereotypes and stories›
I can explain how media shapes what we think about other cultures. Skills · media literacy
A stereotype is an oversimplified idea about a group. Movies, ads and posts can spread stereotypes, or break them. Ask: Who is telling this story? Is it someone from that culture? What's left out?
4Analyzing an issuePositions and reasons›
I can analyze a current issue using an issue-analysis model. Skills · critical thinking
Issue analysis: 1. Identify the issue (as a question). 2. Recognize the different positions. 3. Give reasons for each. 4. Adopt your own position and explain why.

Example

Issue: Should our town rename a street to honour a local First Nations leader?
Positions: yes (recognition, reconciliation) · no (cost, tradition) · maybe (add a second sign).
Your position + your best reason.
5Taking actionMake a difference›
I can plan and take action on an issue I care about. Skills · social involvement
Citizens can act by volunteering, raising awareness, writing letters, fundraising or organizing an event. A good action plan has a goal, steps, people, a timeline and a way to check if it worked.
