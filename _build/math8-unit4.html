Unit 4

Factors & Fractions

Packing boxes, sharing pizza, doubling a recipe, cutting wood to length: they all use multiples, factors and fractions. In this unit you'll find factors and primes, work with fractions and mixed numbers, and add and subtract fractions with the same denominator.

1MultiplesCounting by a number›
I can find multiples of a number. Number 4
Multiples are what you get when you count by a number (skip count): the multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24, 30…

Example Hot dog math

Hot dogs come in packs of 12. Buns come in packs of 8. How many of each do you need so none are left over?
  • Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48
  • Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32
  • 24 is the first number on both lists: 2 packs of hot dogs and 3 packs of buns.
2Factors, primes and compositesNumbers that divide evenly›
I can find factors and tell prime numbers from composite numbers. Number 4
Factors divide into a number with no remainder. A prime number has exactly 2 factors: 1 and itself. A composite number has more than 2.

Example 1 Factors of 24

Find factor pairs: 1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8, 4 × 6. Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24, so 24 is composite.

Example 2 Arranging chairs

You have 24 chairs. You could make rows of 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 or 24, because those are the factors. With 23 chairs (a prime), you can only make 1 long row or 23 rows of 1!
3Fractions and mixed numbersParts of a whole›
I can show proper fractions, mixed numbers and equivalent fractions. Number 5
In 3/4, the bottom (denominator) is how many equal parts, and the top (numerator) is how many you have. A mixed number like 2 1/2 is a whole number plus a fraction.
3/43 of 4 equal parts
1/2 = 2/4Equivalent fractions

Example Equivalent fractions

Multiply or divide the top and bottom by the same number: 1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8. 6/8 ÷ 2 = 3/4.

Example Mixed numbers

A recipe needs 2 1/2 cups of flour: 2 full cups plus half a cup. As halves, that's 5/2 cups.
4Comparing fractionsWhich piece is bigger?›
I can compare and order fractions. Number 6
Same bottom number: the bigger top wins. Same top number: the smaller bottom wins, because the pieces are bigger.

Example 1

3/8 vs 5/8: same size pieces, and 5 pieces is more. 5/8 is greater.

Example 2 Pizza

1/4 of a pizza or 1/6 of the same pizza? Cutting into 4 makes bigger slices than cutting into 6. 1/4 is greater.
5Adding and subtracting fractionsSame denominator›
I can add and subtract fractions with the same denominator. Number 8
When the bottoms match, add or subtract the tops and keep the bottom the same. Simplify if you can.

Example 1 Adding

You walk 3/8 km to a friend's house, then 4/8 km to the store. 3/8 + 4/8 = 7/8 km.

Example 2 Subtracting and simplifying

A board is 5/6 m long. You cut off 2/6 m. 5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2 m left.

Example 3 More than one

3/4 + 3/4 = 6/4 = 1 2/4 = 1 1/2

Factors & Fractions Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Math

Multiples: skip count (6, 12, 18…). Factors: divide in evenly (factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24).

Prime: exactly 2 factors (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13…). Composite: more than 2. 1 is neither.

Fraction: top = how many you have, bottom = equal parts in a whole. Mixed number: 2 1/2 = 5/2.

Equivalent: multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number (1/2 = 2/4 = 4/8).

Compare: same bottom → bigger top wins. Same top → smaller bottom wins.

Add/subtract (same bottom): add or subtract the tops, keep the bottom, simplify. 5/6 − 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2
