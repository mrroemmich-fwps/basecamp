Fresh & Saltwater Systems

Almost all of Earth's water is salty, and only a tiny bit is fresh water we can use. In this unit you'll find out where water is, how it shapes the land, what lives in it, and how people keep it clean, from Alberta's rivers to the oceans.

2Lessons

1Water on EarthSalty, frozen or fresh?›
I can describe where Earth's water is and why fresh water matters. E1
About 97% of Earth's water is salt water in the oceans. Only about 3% is fresh, and most of that is frozen in glaciers and ice caps or deep underground. Less than 1% is easy for people to use.

Example Alberta's water

Most of southern Alberta's river water starts as snow and glacier melt in the Rocky Mountains. Rivers like the Bow and the North Saskatchewan carry it to towns and farms. When glaciers shrink, there is less water in late summer.
2Water qualityTesting and cleaning water›
I can test water quality and explain how distillation makes fresh water. E1
Clear water isn't always clean. Water quality tests check pH (acid or base), dissolved oxygen (for fish), hardness (minerals) and bacteria. Distillation boils water and collects the steam, leaving salt and dirt behind.

Example Boil-water advisory

If tests find harmful bacteria in a town's water, health officials issue a boil-water advisory. Boiling kills bacteria, but it doesn't remove salt or chemicals. That takes distillation or special filters.
3The water cycle and the landErosion, deposition and glaciers›
I can explain the water cycle and how water and ice shape the land. E2
Water moves in a cycle: evaporation → condensation (clouds) → precipitation (rain, snow) → runoff and groundwater → back to the ocean. Along the way, moving water and ice erode (wear away) land and deposit (drop) it somewhere else.

Example 1 Rivers

Fast water in the mountains carves deep valleys. When a river slows down on the prairie, it drops sand and mud, building sandbars and flat floodplains.

Example 2 The Columbia Icefield

The Athabasca Glacier, near the Icefields Parkway, is part of the Columbia Icefield. Glaciers grind rock as they move and leave behind piles of rock called moraines. The Athabasca Glacier has shrunk more than 1.5 km in the last 125 years.
4Life in the waterAdaptations and populations›
I can describe how living things are adapted to fresh and salt water. E1, E3
An adaptation is a feature that helps a living thing survive where it lives. Water animals need ways to breathe, move, find food and handle the salt (or lack of it).
Living thingAdaptationHow it helps
FishGillsTake oxygen out of the water
BeaverWebbed back feet, flat tailSwims and steers well
Water striderLong, light legsWalks on the water's surface
SalmonBody adjusts to salt and fresh waterLives in the ocean, returns to rivers to spawn

Example Populations change

Fish numbers in a lake rise and fall with the seasons (spawning, winter), over years (fishing, drought) and when water quality changes. If oxygen drops in a hot summer, fish can die off.
5People and waterPollution and solutions›
I can explain how people affect water and how technology helps. E4
We use water to drink, farm, make things and have fun. Pollutants from homes, farms and industry can harm water life. Technology like water treatment plants helps, but some problems need people to change what they do.

Fresh & Saltwater Systems Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Science

Earth's water: about 97% salt water, 3% fresh. Most fresh water is frozen in glaciers and ice caps.

Water cycle: evaporation → condensation → precipitation → runoff and groundwater → back to the ocean.

WordMeaning
ErosionWearing away land and carrying it off
DepositionDropping sand, mud or rock in a new place
DistillationBoiling water and collecting the steam to remove salt
AdaptationA feature that helps a living thing survive
MoraineA pile of rock left behind by a glacier

Water quality tests: pH, dissolved oxygen, hardness, bacteria.

Protect water: never pour chemicals or oil down drains, recycle used oil, keep plants along creeks.
