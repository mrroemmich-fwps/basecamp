Fluids, cells, light, machines and water: how science works at home, at work and in the community.

Unit 1 · On the trail now

Mix & Flow of Matter

Water, oil, paint, gasoline, blood and even air are all fluids. In this unit you'll learn how to handle fluids safely, what's really in a mixture, and why some fluids flow fast, some sink and some float.

1Safety first: reading the symbolsWHMIS at work, household symbols at home›
I can read WHMIS and household safety symbols and handle, store and dispose of fluids safely. A1
Before you use any fluid, read the label. The symbol tells you what kind of danger it is.

At home Household product symbols

Cleaners, paint and fuels at home use these symbols. The frame tells you what is dangerous:

💀
PoisonHarmful if swallowed or breathed in. e.g., windshield washer fluid
🔥
FlammableCatches fire easily. e.g., gasoline
✋
CorrosiveBurns skin and eyes. e.g., oven cleaner
💥
Explosive containerCan burst if heated or punctured. e.g., spray cans

Octagon (stop-sign shape) = what's inside is dangerous. Triangle = the container is dangerous. The words CAUTION, WARNING and DANGER tell you how serious it is, from least to most.

At work and school WHMIS pictograms

WHMIS is the safety system used at workplaces across Canada. Its pictograms sit in a red diamond. A few you'll see often:

🔥
FlameFlammable
💀
Skull and crossbonesCan poison quickly
🧪
CorrosionBurns skin, eyes or metal
❗
Exclamation markCan irritate skin or eyes

The pictures here are a simple guide. Learn the real pictograms from the CCOHS link in Resources.

Safe handling Use, store, dispose

  • Use: read the label, wear goggles and gloves when told to, work with fresh air.
  • Store: in the original container, closed tight, away from heat and out of reach of kids.
  • Dispose: never pour chemicals down the drain. Take them to a household hazardous waste drop-off.
  • Never mix cleaners. Bleach mixed with ammonia makes a toxic gas.

2Pure substances, mixtures and solutionsWhat's really in there?›
I can tell the difference between pure substances, mixtures and solutions. A2
A pure substance has only one kind of particle. A mixture has two or more kinds.
Pure substanceOne kind of particle. e.g., pure water, sugar
Mechanical mixtureYou can see the different parts. e.g., trail mix, salad
SolutionMixed so evenly it looks like one thing. e.g., salt water, juice

Words to know

  • Solute: the thing that dissolves (salt, sugar, drink crystals).
  • Solvent: the thing it dissolves into (usually water).
  • Dissolve: break into particles so small you can't see them anymore.

Example Making a sports drink

Powder (solute) + water (solvent) → stir → the powder disappears and the colour spreads evenly. That's a solution. If you add ice cubes, you can see them, so the drink-plus-ice is a mechanical mixture.

3Solubility and concentrationHow much will dissolve? How strong is it?›
I can explain solubility and concentration. A2
Solubility is how much solute can dissolve. Concentration is how much solute is in a certain amount of solution: concentration = solute ÷ volume.

Example 1 Which is stronger?

Cup A: 20 g of drink crystals in 250 mL. Cup B: 10 g in 250 mL.
  1. Same amount of water, but A has more crystals.
  2. Cup A is more concentrated (stronger taste, darker colour).

Example 2 Calculating concentration

30 g of salt in 100 mL of water: 30 ÷ 100 = 0.3 g/mL. Add 100 mL more water: 30 ÷ 200 = 0.15 g/mL. Adding water dilutes it.

Example 3 Saturated

Keep adding sugar to cold water and it eventually piles up on the bottom. The solution is saturated: no more will dissolve. Warm the water and more dissolves, because heat usually increases solubility.

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Taste Test Lab

With an adult, make 3 cups of drink mix with different amounts of powder in the same amount of water. Rank them by concentration, then calculate g/mL for each.

4Viscosity and flow rateWhy ketchup is slow and water is fast›
I can describe viscosity and how temperature changes flow rate. A3, A4
Viscosity is a fluid's resistance to flowing. High viscosity = thick and slow (honey). Low viscosity = thin and fast (water). Flow rate is how fast it moves.

Example 1 Ranking fluids

From fastest flow to slowest: water → vegetable oil → dish soap → ketchup → honey

Example 2 Temperature

Cold honey barely moves. Warm it up and it pours easily. Heating a liquid usually lowers its viscosity, so it flows faster.

Example 3 On the job: motor oil

Motor oil labelled 5W-30 is made to stay thin in an Alberta winter ("W" = winter) so the engine can start, but not get too thin when the engine is hot. Mechanics choose oil by its viscosity.

Quick check

🧭

5Density and buoyancySink or float?›
I can use density to explain why things sink or float. A3
Density is how much mass is packed into a space: density = mass ÷ volume. Water's density is 1 g/mL. Less dense than water → floats. More dense → sinks.
Density columnLess dense liquids float on top

Example 1 Calculating density

A rock has a mass of 60 g and a volume of 20 mL. 60 ÷ 20 = 3 g/mL. That's more than 1, so it sinks.

Example 2 Ice

Ice is about 0.9 g/mL, a little less dense than water. That's why ice floats and lakes freeze from the top down, so fish survive the winter underneath.

Example 3 Buoyancy

Buoyancy is the upward push from a fluid. A life jacket is full of low-density foam, so it adds lots of buoyancy and keeps you floating.

Quick check

🧭
Expedition: Density Tower

With an adult, build a density tower in a clear glass using 3 or 4 liquids (honey, dish soap, water, oil). Drop in small objects and explain where each one stops.

6Pressure and fluid technologyTires, hoses, pumps and pipelines›
I can explain fluid pressure and the technology that uses it. A3, A4
Pressure is force pushing on an area: pressure = force ÷ area. Squeezing a fluid into a small space raises its pressure.

Example 1 Garden hose

Put your thumb over the end of a hose and the water sprays farther. A smaller opening means higher pressure.

Example 2 Tire pressure

Air pumped into a tire pushes outward on the inside. Low tire pressure in cold weather makes a car harder to steer and burns more gas. Tire pressure is measured in kPa or psi.

Example 3 On the job: pumps and pipelines

Alberta moves oil, natural gas and water through pipelines. Pumps raise the pressure to push fluids long distances. Thicker (more viscous) oil needs more pressure or heating to flow. Pipeline operators, welders and engineers all use this science every day.

Mix & Flow of Matter Cheat Sheet · Grade 8 Science

Safety symbols at home: the symbol shows the type of danger (poison, flammable, corrosive, explosive). Octagon = contents are dangerous. Triangle = container is dangerous. Caution → Warning → Danger (least to most serious).

WHMIS (at work): pictograms in a red diamond. Read the label and safety data sheet before using any product.

WordMeaning
Pure substanceOnly one kind of particle (pure water, sugar)
Mechanical mixtureYou can see the different parts (trail mix)
SolutionEvenly mixed; looks like one thing (salt water)
Solute / solventThe part that dissolves / what it dissolves into
SaturatedNo more solute can dissolve
ViscosityResistance to flowing (honey = high, water = low)
BuoyancyThe upward push from a fluid

Formulas: concentration = solute ÷ volume · density = mass ÷ volume (water = 1 g/mL; less than 1 floats) · pressure = force ÷ area

Temperature: warmer liquids usually flow faster and dissolve more.
